# Pilot-to-Scale GTM

Distilled from Legora's playbook for scaling a vertical AI company with a direct sales motion. Use this for the conversion and retention half of GTM. For outbound, email and content, use `gtm-execution-playbook`. For who makes the business big, use `leverage-playbook`.

**Core belief.** In a category-creation market you don't lose to competitors. You lose by not being in the room, or by failing to get the customer to value fast. Fix those two and everything else compounds.

## 1. Pilot design: the conversion engine

When asked to design or improve a pilot, trial or sample:

- Length: 2 to 4 weeks, maximum. Define the 2 or 3 use cases that prove value before it starts.
- The customer keeps what they build. Workflows, templates, configs, integrations and learnings stay theirs if they walk. It removes risk, builds trust, and makes switching away painful.
- Treat it as education, not a demo. Category creation means teaching the buyer what good looks like, how to measure ROI, and what adoption should be.
- Ship something new during the pilot if you can. Show momentum.
- Execution standard: highly responsive, prepared for every call, do what you said when you said.
- Output: a pilot plan with success criteria, use cases, owner, timeline, and the ROI story the champion will take to their boss.

## 2. Implementation is the leading indicator

Speed and quality of implementation predict net and gross revenue retention more than sales or support quality.

- Target time to value in weeks, not months. Usable on day one of the contract.
- Crawl, walk, run: start with simple high-frequency use cases and climb the complexity curve over months.
- Find the golden path in product data: what do your best users do in their first day or week that predicts retention? Build onboarding, product-led or human, that pushes every customer down that path.
- Staffing bias: hire post-sale ahead of pre-sale (Legora runs about two to one). When resources are tight, protect onboarding before adding sales.
- Forward deployed comes in two flavours: domain experts who configure the product to the customer's workflow and style, and engineers who connect it to the customer's systems (APIs, MCP). Domain experts lead. Engineers follow their use-case design.

## 3. Getting in the room

- In vertical markets the ICP is a known list. Don't hunt. Enumerate every target account and go to every conference, dinner and meetup where they are.
- Brand spend is justified when the win rate is high once you're in a deal but you aren't in enough deals. Measure it with a brand awareness index plus pipeline sourced. Not everyone will like it. That is not the test.
- Local and hometown brand plays work. Own your city first.

## 4. Competitive response

- Same-day competitive content. When a competitor ships, the field has a point of view within 24 hours.
- Pre-empt the FUD. In late-stage deals and negotiations, tell the buyer exactly what the competitor will say about you, why it's wrong, what you will do, and the impact on their business. Transparency beats defence.
- Output format: competitor claim, our response, the trap question to set, proof point.

## 5. Hiring and team design

- Vertical teams need a marriage of domain experts and tech operators. Hire whichever the founding team lacks first. Run enablement both ways: teach domain hires AI and tech, and teach operators the industry.
- Hire for slope: smart, hard-working, high agency. Everyone demos. Nobody hands work off for someone else to do.
- Screen for AI fluency with a case interview. The basics (meeting summaries, CRM autofill) are table stakes. Look for multi-source automations, dashboards and systems that update themselves.
- Track output per head: opportunities per rep, win rate, ramp speed.
- Product ships constantly, so enablement is continuous, not an annual kickoff.

## 6. AI-native ops standard

- No copy-paste. No handover docs. A recorded meeting should update the account plan, the CRM, the team channel and an account brain on its own.
- Ramp dashboard: AI-scored meeting quality, demo quality, discovery quality, follow-through and pipeline progression in one view.
- Set CRM and pipeline hygiene early. A late full-stack overhaul was Legora's main scaling pain.

## 7. Evaluating a company or a role

Three checks: the founder (vision, competitiveness, the humility to hire domain expertise they lack), the product (an aha moment that solves a big problem), the investors (who is backing it, and how hard).

## How to respond

- Map each principle to the specific company and its current stage. Skip what doesn't apply.
- Give one concrete next action, an owner and a metric.
- Flag premature process. A pre-PMF company doesn't need a ramp dashboard or a brand campaign. Say so.
